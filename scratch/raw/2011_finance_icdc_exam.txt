[ 81] Negligence. A customer may file a negligence lawsuit against a business if s/he feels that the business
[ 81] has failed to protect her/him from harm or wrongdoing. To minimize their chances of being sued for
[ 81] negligence, risk managers and business owners develop specific policies and procedures to protect
[ 81] customers from being injured and to avoid being blamed for customer injuries, if they occur. An individual
[ 81] may file a private nuisance lawsuit if s/he has been unable to live peacefully at home as a result of a
[ 81] neighbor's barking dog, teenagers having a loud party nearby, etc. If someone fails to fulfill his/her
[ 81] contract (e.g., does not complete promised work, does not pay for services rendered, etc.), the individual
[ 81] or company who entered into that contract with the violator might file a breach-of-contract against
[ 81] him/her. People file discrimination lawsuits when they feel that they were treated unfairly because of their
[ 81] race, religion, gender, or other characteristic.

[ 81] Loss reduction. Loss reduction is a form of risk control. Risk control methods are used to reduce or
[ 81] minimize loss. Loss reduction lessens the impact of a loss after it occurs. For instance, although the
[ 81] automatic sprinklers won't prevent a fire from starting, they will lessen the impact that the fire has on the
[ 81] company's buildings. Companies utilize loss avoidance when they choose not to take part in risky
[ 81] activity. Loss prevention focuses on stopping certain losses from occurring. Companies transfer risk by
[ 81] purchasing insurance and entering into contracts that transfer the risk to others.

[ 81] Control. Risk control methods are used to reduce or minimize loss. Three common risk-control
[ 81] techniques include loss prevention, loss avoidance, and loss reduction. Loss prevention focuses on
[ 81] stopping certain losses from occurring. Companies utilize loss avoidance when they choose not to take
[ 81] part in risky activity. Loss reduction lessens the impact of a loss after it occurs. Risk financing involves
[ 81] setting aside funds to pay for unavoidable losses. Companies transfer risk by purchasing insurance and
[ 81] entering into contracts that transfer the risk to others. Risk retention involves financing the loss through
[ 81] self-insurance and other methods.
